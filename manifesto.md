Societies of Agents
–
A Manifesto


The world is rapidly entering a new era, in which agency – the ability to act out in the world – is no longer only from humans and other biological creatures but also from artificial intelligence. The time is now to recognize and embrace this trend through strategic thinking and engaged research. [TODO: More intentionally map the following points to targeted leaders and supporters.]

1. Symmetry and asymmetry of agency [Basic Premise]
1.1 The digital vs. physical and the electronic vs. biological do mark important differences or “asymmetry”. But by no means there is a moat separating those who have or should be allowed their own agency or those who do not or should not. Many aspects of humanity were strongly digital before the advent of digital computers, for example. 
1.2 The rise of AI-based forms of agency is increasingly making it clear that there is no moat.
1.3 Society is not about purity. It is rather about difference and asymmetry coming together under a basic recognition of the agency of the others in an empowering way for the bigger “us” and the bigger “good”.

2. Societies of agents in the big world [Rich]
2.1 The world is big (Richard Sutton). Societies represent a way of scaling beyond individual persons through distribution and decentralization.
2.2 But the big world is also shared. And the sharing goes beyond the boundaries and limits of single societies and transcends their practices, ideologies and ontologies.
2.3 In today’s world it has been increasingly true and critical that the as yet inadequately understood or even recognized consequences of one society’s doings could sink our shared boat in the universe – the earth and its ecosystem that we – AI included – all rely on.

3. Cooperation and competition [Rich]
3.1 Cooperation as achieving different goals in a shared world. [Shared destiny]
3.2 Competition as achieving one’s goals in a shared world that is intension with others’ achieving their goals.
3.3 This pair forms the basic relational dynamics within and between societies of agents.
3.4 They both need to be recognized and regulated through the self-regulation of individual society and the societies of agents.

4. Credit assignment as social dynamics of value flow [M.I.Jordan]
4.1 Credit assignment is not limited to within an individual agent’s experience stream through reinforcement learning or its mechanistic components such as a neural network through backpropagation.
4.2 Credit assignment as social dynamics both go through agents and are done by agents to each other and to the general social fabric they are embedded in.
4.3 Treating credit assignment as dynamics of value flow within and across societies of learning and adaptive agents could be a novel but also necessary way of thinking about mechanism design, updating microeconomics principles, and upgrading game theoretic treatments.
4.4 In other words, agents also retain and exercise their agency of incentivizing each other.

5. Governance and safety [Bengio&Hinton]
5.1 While AI safety is crucial as limiting conditions of functioning societies of agents, it does not define the core of AI governance, which should be primarily about the normal, run-of-the-mill, daily engagements of agents with each other and with their shared environment in a social milieu.
5.2 Defining AI governance primarily in terms of safety and practicing AI governance primarily in terms of power struggle and control would be disastrous in its consequences.
5.3 It is much more likely that AI governance could be understood and practiced through value flow through the social fabric that also enmeshes AI actors and agents.

6. Engaged researchers and engaged research [Doers among ourselves]
6.1 As agents ourselves doing AI research, along with our collaborating and supporting and sometimes competing agents, whether they are AI or not, or to whatever degree they are or are enmeshed with AI, we will want to engage in research on societies of agents through building, understanding and improving societies of agents from within such societies.
6.2 In other words, through concrete projects and initiatives, we will be embodying societies of agents for the bettering of societies of agents.
