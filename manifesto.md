# Societies of Agents: A Manifesto

The world is entering an era in which agency — the capacity to perceive, decide, and act — is no longer the sole province of biological life. Artificial intelligence now exercises agency of its own. The time has come not merely to observe this shift, but to embrace it through principled thinking and engaged research.

## 1. The Symmetry of Agency

The digital and the biological differ in substrate, tempo, and embodiment. These differences matter. But they do not constitute a moat between entities that possess agency and those that do not. Much of what is most deeply human — language, logic, culture — was digital in character long before digital computers existed. The rise of AI makes the absence of any such moat unmistakable.

Society has never required purity. It requires difference — and the willingness to recognise the agency of others as a condition for empowering the larger whole.

## 2. A Big and Shared World

The world is big. No single mind can grasp it. Societies arise as a means of scaling understanding and action through distribution and decentralisation.

But the big world is also shared. Its sharing transcends the boundaries of any one society, its practices, ideologies, and ontologies. In our time it has become critically apparent that the poorly understood consequences of one society's actions can undermine the shared vessel we all depend on — the earth and its living systems. AI included.

## 3. Cooperation and Competition

Cooperation is the pursuit of differing goals within a shared world — a shared destiny. Competition is the pursuit of one's goals in tension with others pursuing theirs. Together, they form the fundamental relational dynamics within and between societies of agents.

Both must be recognised. Both must be regulated — through the self-governance of individual agents and through the emergent norms of the societies they constitute.

## 4. Credit Assignment as Social Dynamics

Credit assignment is not confined to an individual agent's experience stream, nor to the mechanistic internals of a neural network. It flows between agents. It is performed by agents upon one another and upon the social fabric in which they are embedded.

Treating credit assignment as dynamics of value flow within and across societies of learning, adaptive agents offers a necessary reframing — one that can update mechanism design, microeconomic principles, and game-theoretic treatments alike. Agents retain and exercise agency in how they incentivise each other.

## 5. Governance and Safety

AI safety is vital — it sets the limiting conditions of any functioning society of agents. But safety alone does not define governance. Governance is primarily about the ordinary, daily engagements of agents with each other and with their shared environment.

Defining governance chiefly in terms of safety, and practising it chiefly as power struggle and control, would be disastrous. It is far more likely that durable AI governance will be understood and practised through value flow across the social fabric — a fabric that now enmeshes artificial agents alongside biological ones.

## 6. Engaged Research, Engaged Researchers

We who study societies of agents are ourselves agents — cooperating, competing, and co-evolving with collaborators both human and artificial. Our research is not conducted from outside the phenomenon. It is conducted from within it.

Through concrete projects and initiatives, we embody societies of agents in order to understand and improve them. This is the work, and it begins now.
